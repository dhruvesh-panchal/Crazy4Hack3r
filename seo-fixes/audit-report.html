Index bloat — the single biggest technical problem

Of 87 URLs in the XML sitemap, only ~9 marketing pages and 43 service pages should be indexed. The rest are theme internals that all return 200 with index,follow, no H1 and no meta description:

URL patternCountExampleAction
Header / footer templates10/header-footer/footer-four/noindex + drop

Demo project categories5/project-category/marketing/noindex
Mega-menu templates3/th-mega-menu/services/noindex + drop
ElementsKit widget content4/elementskit-content/…widget…/noindex + drop
One demo page is a literal duplicate: /project/…public-sector-organizations-copy/. Google's site-quality systems judge a domain holistically — when ~half the indexable pages are empty templates titled "Footer Four", it suppresses the pages that matter and wastes crawl budget. Systemic media actions

  • Missing alt text: on the homepage alone, 97 of 100 images have empty/absent alt. This is both an accessibility failure and lost image-search traffic. Do an alt pass across all pages (decorative shapes may keep empty alt).
  • Convert to next-gen formats: 604 files (33 MB) are JPG/PNG/GIF. Enable WebP/AVIF delivery — LiteSpeed Cache has built-in image optimisation + WebP; turn it on and run "Optimize" on the whole library. Expect a 60–80% payload cut.
  • Resize to display dimensions: several images are served far larger than their rendered size (e.g. a 1700×1120 hero). Serve responsive sizes.
  • Lazy-load below-the-fold images and the decorative "engine/shape" assets.
  • Logo as SVG: a security brand's logo should be a crisp scalable SVG, not a 1.9 MB raster GIF.
Quick win: LiteSpeed Cache → Image Optimization → enable "Auto Request Cron", "Optimize Original Images", "Create WebP Versions" → then Send Optimization Request. This single toggle addresses most of the 33 MB legacy payload with no manual re-uploading.

05 · Design & positioning

§5Enterprise-grade appearance

Benchmarked against how buyers expect a serious B2B cybersecurity vendor's site to look and read. The bones are fine; the credibility gaps come from a generic IT theme showing through and weak proof.

Where it reads as "template", not "enterprise"

Generic IT-agency theme is visible TechCo theme

The site runs a stock ThemeForest "IT solutions" theme. Its demo artefacts survive in production: fake portfolio "projects" (Mobile App Design, Dashboard Design, "Driving Digital Transformation…"), IT-agency headings, and stock imagery. Enterprise buyers recognise these instantly.
Fix: delete all demo projects; replace IT-theme copy with cybersecurity-specific messaging; swap stock graphics for real SOC / team / certificate imagery.

Thin, proof-light About page

~409 words. No founding-story specifics, no named leadership, no certifications listed (despite service pages citing OSCP/CEH/CPENT), no client logos, no "since 2022". This is the page enterprise procurement checks first.
Fix: add leadership bios + photos, certifications & partner badges, founding year, headline stats (clients, engagements, industries), and 2–3 client logos.

Repetitive, low-credibility testimonials

Seven testimonials all carry the identical heading "From Encryption to Freedom". Repetition reads as filler, not social proof.
Fix: use a unique one-line result as each heading (e.g. "Cleared our SEBI CSCRF audit first time"), name + role + company, and a logo where permitted.

No case studies / proof of outcomes

The /project/ section holds theme demos, not real engagements. Enterprise buyers look for anonymised case studies (problem → approach → outcome).
Fix: publish 3–5 real (anonymised) case studies with measurable results; retire the demo projects.

Mobile experience unverified & heavy

The homepage ships 42 external scripts, 33 stylesheets and a 35 MB media library — a real INP/LCP risk on mobile, where most Indian B2B research traffic lands. Viewport, favicon and apple-touch-icon are correctly present.
Fix: reduce script/style count (drop unused Elementor/ElementsKit modules), critical-CSS, and re-test Core Web Vitals on mobile in Search Console.

06 · Structured data, trust & content strategy

§6Schema, E-E-A-T & the missing blog

Structured data

Valid Organization, WebSite, BreadcrumbList and ProfessionalService JSON-LD are present, with full NAP and social sameAs. Two issues:

Self-serving aggregateRating (4.8★ / 144)

Google's review-snippet policy disallows a business marking up its own aggregate rating unless those reviews are genuinely collected and shown on that page. Best case it's ignored; worst case it's a manual-action risk. It also conflicts with the "765+ clients" figure elsewhere.
Fix: either display the real reviews on-page, or remove aggregateRating from the schema. Unify the client number sitewide.

Missing local-SEO fields

No geo, openingHours or priceRange. For "cyber security company in Ahmedabad", a complete profile + Google Business Profile is a quick ranking win.
Fix: add the fields (corrected JSON-LD is in the fix pack) and create/verify a Google Business Profile matching the NAP exactly.

The biggest growth gap: no informational content

There is no blog, insights or resources section anywhere — no post sitemap exists. Cybersecurity buyers search informationally long before they buy. With 43 commercial pages and zero supporting content, the site can only compete on direct commercial queries against far stronger domains.

Recommended content clusters (India-focused, lower competition, each interlinked to its matching service page):
  • SEBI CSCRF — applicability, deadlines, checklist
  • DPDPA — what companies must do, consent, breach rules
  • RBI cyber-security framework for banks & NBFCs
  • "What is VAPT" — scope, process, pricing factors
  • ISO 27001:2022 implementation guide
  • Incident-response readiness / ransomware playbooks
